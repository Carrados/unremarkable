A real job, start to finish: one folder in, one finished piece out.

This is a worked example. The job is real: Blind Ant's own publishing agent, the one Cloud Dancer is modelled on, did it for blind-ant.com on 5 November 2026, and you can open the result. Nothing on this page publishes anything.

## The request

Charlotte Joanne, who runs blind-ant.com, put one folder in Blind Ant's in-tray. There was no form, no fields and no instructions, just what she already had:

- Two press releases, as Word documents
- One picture, the mark of an invented company

The agent checks the in-tray twice a day.

## The result

A new piece of three pages: the piece itself and the two releases, with headings, lists, links, a described picture, the licence, and a Markdown version of every page for agents. Open it on your own device, the way you would check a change to your own site.

[Red DOG, on blind-ant.com](https://blind-ant.com/pieces/red-dog/)

## What the agent reported

Every job ends with a receipt, written for the owner. This one said:

- **Her words:** unchanged. The two headlines became the page titles, her section headings became headings, her lists became lists, and "A realistic order of work" became a numbered list. The email address in the text became a link.
- **Typos fixed:** none found.
- **Lines the agent wrote itself:** the piece's description, two paragraphs about it, a section on what is invented and what is real, and the picture's alt text and description, each quoted word for word in the receipt so she could check them.
- **Checks:** one main heading on every page, no sideways scrolling on a phone or a desktop, and nothing changed but the new piece.
- **What it couldn't check:** "NOT VERIFIED: I could not fetch the live page." The next morning's run checked that the site had been built and published, and said so.
- **The question:** "Please confirm reddog@blind-ant.com is a mailbox that exists." The owner answered yes: blind-ant.com catches every address sent to it, so the mailbox works.

## The decision

The receipt ended: "Say 'pull it' to take the Piece down." The owner decides. The agent never has the last word.

Blind Ant runs its agent in live mode, because a design fiction studio can afford to publish first and pull later. Your site starts the other way round: with Cloud Dancer, the same job stops at a private copy of your site, and nothing reaches your visitors until you say so. If you come to trust it, you can choose live mode for the small things.

## What it shows

- You give us what you have, in the form you have it. Nobody copies it into fields.
- Your words stay yours. Anything the agent writes is set out for you to check.
- The agent says what it couldn't do, and asks what it doesn't know, instead of guessing.
- You decide what stays live.
