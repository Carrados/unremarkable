Where your website lives, what keeps it running, and what that costs you.

Where your website lives

Where is my website?

In three places, all in your name. Its files are in your own GitHub organisation. It is live on Cloudflare, in your own account. And its address, the domain, is registered to you.

We work inside those accounts as a member, and you can remove us whenever you like. That is the whole of our hold on your website.

What does GitHub do?

GitHub keeps your website's files: every page, every picture, and the short build script that turns them into a website. They sit in a repository, a folder that remembers every change ever made, who made it and why, so any earlier version can be brought back.

You never need to open it. You ask for changes in your in-tray, and we make them. GitHub is what lets you take the whole website with you, and lets any developer or agent you choose carry on where we left off.

What does Cloudflare do?

Cloudflare puts your website in front of visitors, from a network that spans the world, so a page opens quickly wherever someone is. There is no server of yours to update, patch or keep running. That is Cloudflare's job.

Your forms run on Cloudflare as small programs called Workers. Your domain's settings live there too, in the same account.

How does a change go live?

Every change waits on a private copy of your site first, where you try it. When you say publish, the change is added to your repository, Cloudflare builds the website from it, and the new version is live within a couple of minutes. Nothing reaches your visitors until you say so.

What it costs you

Does GitHub cost anything?

No. GitHub's free plan for organisations includes private repositories and as many members as you need, which is everything your website uses. Your files stay private to the people you allow. The website itself is public, as a website should be.

Is the hosting really free?

For most sites, yes. Cloudflare serves plain files, meaning your pages, pictures and stylesheet, free and without limit. We build your pages as plain files, so a visit costs nothing, however many people come.

Only code counts: your forms, and anything else that runs a Worker. The free plan allows 100,000 of those a day, more than the forms on most organisations' sites will ever use.

What if my forms get very busy?

Then Cloudflare's paid plan, from US$5 a month for your whole account, with 10 million requests to your code included each month. Beyond that, you pay a little for what you use: a month with 15 million requests, each taking about 7 milliseconds of processing, would cost about US$8 in all. Cloudflare's pricing page, listed under "Where these figures come from", has the exact rates.

We watch your usage, and tell you when we recommend moving to the paid plan, so the move is your decision.

If a free allowance does run out, nothing is bought for you. Your forms pause until the next day, and your pages carry on, because they are plain files.

What does my domain cost?

Your domain is registered with Cloudflare at the registry's own price, with no markup. Most cost £10 to £40 a year, depending on the ending. We check the price, and the price to renew it, with you before you buy.

Free hosting doesn't make the address free. It renews every year, and it is yours for as long as you renew it.

Are these costs in your monthly price?

No, and that is deliberate. Our monthly price pays for changing and looking after your website. Anything GitHub, Cloudflare or your email provider charges is charged to you directly, at their price. We never mark it up, because we never hold it.

If you leave

Can I move my website somewhere else?

Yes. You already have everything: the files, the accounts and the domain, with a handover note that another developer or agent can follow. Your pages are plain HTML and can go to any host. Your forms are written for Cloudflare, and would need adapting.

A domain registered with Cloudflare uses Cloudflare to direct its visitors. You can point it at a new host whenever you like and keep it with Cloudflare. To manage it somewhere else entirely, you move the domain to another registrar.

Can I keep changing my website after I leave?

Yes, two ways. Any developer, or an AI agent you trust, can follow the handover note. Or you can take your own copy of our publishing agent, set up in your own Claude account, and carry on asking for changes in your in-tray as before. That is a one-off price, on Prices.
